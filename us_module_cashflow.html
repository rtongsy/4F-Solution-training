4F Solution — US Lesson 2: Lever One — reducing outflows

Debt restructuring — the US toolkit

When the tracking data reveals high-interest debt, these are the tools available in the US context. The discipline check from Module 3 applies to all of them: never restructure debt without first confirming the spending behaviour that created it has changed.

Tools that restructure rate
Personal debt consolidation loan
A fixed-rate unsecured loan that retires multiple high-rate balances into a single lower-rate payment. Available through banks, credit unions, and online lenders. Best for clients with good credit (720+) carrying $5,000–$40,000 in credit card debt. The payment is fixed and the debt remains unsecured — the client's home is not at risk.
Typical APR: 8–20% vs credit card rates of 20–28% — meaningful monthly savings
Discipline check: confirms the spending behavior is resolved before applying. Monthly payment must fit comfortably within the revised cash flow plan.
Balance transfer credit card (0% intro APR)
Many US issuers offer 0% APR for 12–21 months on transferred balances. A client who can realistically retire the balance within the promotional window gets an interest-free window. Balance transfer fee is typically 3–5% of the transferred amount — still well below months of high-rate interest. After the promotional period, the rate reverts — often higher than the original card.
Window: 12–21 months; transfer fee: 3–5% of balance
Only appropriate for clients whose discipline problem is genuinely resolved. Using a 0% window to spend more creates a compounding problem at reversal.
Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC)
A revolving line of credit secured against home equity. US HELOCs typically have variable rates tied to prime — currently significantly below credit card rates. Consolidating $20,000+ in credit card debt into a HELOC can reduce monthly interest by $300–$500. The critical distinction: credit card debt is unsecured — a HELOC converts it to a loan secured by the client's home. This is not an upgrade in risk-free terms; it is a trade.
Rate: typically Prime + 1–3% (currently far below credit card rates)
Variable rate risk: if rates rise significantly, the payment increases. Discipline check is non-negotiable here — a HELOC with remaining credit card spending capacity is a high-risk combination.
Cash-out mortgage refinance
Refinancing an existing mortgage for more than the current balance and taking the difference as cash — used to retire high-interest debt. In rate environments where mortgage rates are competitive, this can dramatically reduce total monthly debt payments. The trade-off: extending the mortgage term and converting short-term debt into a 15–30-year obligation against the home. Best analyzed with a mortgage professional calculating total cost over the life of the loan — not just the monthly payment reduction.
Must compare: total interest over life of loan — not just monthly payment change
Refer to a licensed mortgage professional. This is not a casual recommendation — it requires a full refinance analysis and disclosure process.
The discipline check — always
The correct sequencing is: tracking → alignment conversation → spending behaviour shift confirmed → then restructuring. A client whose discipline problem is unresolved will re-accumulate the credit card debt and now also carry the restructured obligation. The professional's job is to surface this risk clearly — not to avoid the debt conversation, but to time it correctly.
4F Solution — US Lesson 3: Lever One continued — when debt is overwhelming

When restructuring is not enough — US legal options

Sometimes the total debt load is beyond what restructuring can address. These are the formal legal tools in the US context — each has real consequences and each requires a referral to the right professional.

Nonprofit credit counseling / Debt Management Plan (DMP)
For clients who can repay over 3–5 years with reduced interest rates
A nonprofit credit counseling agency negotiates reduced interest rates with creditors and consolidates payments into a single monthly payment over 3–5 years. Not a debt forgiveness option — the client repays 100% of what is owed, just at a lower rate with a structured timeline. Best for clients with steady income who need a structured framework rather than a legal process.
Full repayment — no forgiveness Reduced interest rates Minor credit impact
Refer only to NFCC-member nonprofit agencies. Avoid for-profit debt settlement companies claiming similar services — they are not the same.
Chapter 13 Bankruptcy — Reorganization
For clients with regular income who want to keep assets (especially a home) and repay under court supervision over 3–5 years
A federally supervised repayment plan that restructures debt obligations over 3–5 years. The client retains most assets — including their home, which is critical for clients facing foreclosure. At completion, remaining unsecured debt is discharged. Stops all collection actions and foreclosure proceedings immediately upon filing.
Assets generally retained Foreclosure stays halted Credit impact: 7 years
Chapter 7 Bankruptcy — Liquidation
For clients who cannot repay and pass the means test — discharges most unsecured debt within 3–6 months
The fastest form of bankruptcy protection — most unsecured debt (credit cards, medical bills, personal loans) is discharged within 3–6 months. Non-exempt assets may be liquidated. Exemptions vary by state — homestead exemption in some states fully protects a primary residence. Requires passing a means test (income below state median, or disposable income test). Student loans, child support, and recent taxes are not dischargeable.
Non-exempt assets liquidated Credit impact: 10 years State exemptions vary widely
The correct referral — always a bankruptcy attorney
Only a licensed attorney can file bankruptcy in the US. Bankruptcy attorneys typically offer a free or low-cost initial consultation. They are not debt collectors — they are legal professionals whose job is to find the best outcome for the client. Avoid debt settlement companies that promise to negotiate lump-sum forgiveness — most operate on high fees and do not produce the protections of a formal legal process.
How to have this conversation
"What I'm seeing in your numbers is that the debt has reached a point where reducing the rate alone is unlikely to change the outcome — the balance is too large relative to what's available to put toward it. There are legal options specifically designed for this situation. I'd like to refer you to a bankruptcy attorney — most offer a free consultation, and they are the right person to walk you through what's actually available. Getting to the other side of this is what makes the rest of the plan possible."
4F Solution — US Lesson 4: Lever One continued — cost reduction programs

Federal programs that reduce the cost of living

Beyond debt restructuring, several federal programs directly reduce what qualifying clients pay out of pocket — creating cash flow through lower expenses rather than higher income. These are not strategies to implement. They are programs to surface and refer.

ACA Marketplace Health Insurance Subsidies
Premium Tax Credits (PTCs) are available to individuals and families whose household income falls between 100% and 400% of the federal poverty level — and in many cases above 400% under current expanded subsidy provisions. For clients who are self-employed, between jobs, or whose employer does not offer affordable coverage, this can reduce monthly health insurance premiums by hundreds of dollars. Health insurance is frequently the largest single line item discovered in client tracking data that can be materially reduced through program access.
Eligibility: income-based; applied through healthcare.gov during open enrollment or qualifying life events
Clients must apply through the Marketplace — not directly through an insurer — to access the subsidy. Many eligible Americans pay full price because no one has walked them through the process.
LIHEAP — Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program
Federal block grant program that helps low-income households cover heating and cooling costs. Administered by states — benefit amounts and eligibility thresholds vary significantly. For clients whose utility costs appear as an unexpected line item in tracking data, LIHEAP can provide direct bill reduction or credits. Many eligible households do not know the program exists or believe they do not qualify because income thresholds are higher than they assume.
Eligibility: varies by state; typically income at or below 150% of poverty level
Direct clients to Benefits.gov or their state energy office to check eligibility and apply. State programs supplement federal LIHEAP in many jurisdictions.
SNAP — Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program
Federal food assistance program for low-income households. For qualifying clients, SNAP directly reduces food expenditures — one of the largest spending categories that clients consistently underestimate during tracking. Average benefit for a family of four is meaningful monthly relief. Many working families qualify and do not know it, particularly those with fluctuating income or recent income changes.
Average benefit: $230–$740/month depending on household size and income
Applied through the state SNAP agency. Benefits.gov lists state contacts. Not means-tested at a single point — recent income changes affect eligibility in the client's favor.
State programs
Every state has additional benefit programs that reduce the cost of living — Medicaid expansion, prescription drug assistance, rental assistance, childcare subsidies, and more. These vary significantly by state and are covered in the state supplement materials. Complete the supplement for every state in which you are licensed before working with clients in that state.
4F Solution — US Lesson 5: Lever Two — earning more

Increasing inflows — entitlements and income generation

Two distinct approaches to putting more money in the client's hands. First: money they are already entitled to but not receiving. Second: income they could be generating but have not considered.

Child Tax Credit (CTC)

Phases out beginning at $200,000 AGI (single) and $400,000 (married filing jointly). The refundable Additional Child Tax Credit portion can result in a refund even when tax liability is zero. Requires filing — a non-filing client with children receives nothing.

Fully refundable — if the credit exceeds the client's tax liability, the IRS sends the difference as a refund. One of the most significant cash payments available to working-income clients. IRS estimates that approximately 20% of eligible taxpayers do not claim it. Filing is the trigger — the IRS will not contact non-filers to notify them of eligibility.

Active income generation — what the client can create

Government programs surface money the client is entitled to but not receiving. Income generation is a different conversation — it is about helping the client see options they have not considered. The professional's role is to ask the question and open the door, not to implement the strategy.

💼 Skills and consulting
Freelance, contract, or consulting work alongside employment. Many clients have expertise with real market value outside their current role — they simply have not thought of it as a cash flow lever.
🏠 Asset utilization
Renting a spare room, short-term rental, storage space, or vehicle. Underutilized assets sitting idle while the client carries high-interest debt is a direct misallocation.
📦 Gig and platform work
Rideshare, delivery, task platforms. Flexible hours that fit around employment. Not a long-term strategy — a short-term cash flow lever to retire debt faster during the discipline-resolved phase.
🛒 Selling unused assets
Vehicles, equipment, electronics, collections. One-time cash generation that can make a material difference when directed toward high-rate debt reduction.
Income generation question
"Are there skills, assets, or time you have that you are not currently converting to income?"
Opens the conversation without prescribing. Lets the client identify their own options. The professional surfaces the possibility — the client decides what is realistic.
4F Solution — US Lesson 6: Discovery questions

The questions that surface US cash flow opportunities

These questions are asked during discovery — not after. Each one opens a category of opportunity that would otherwise remain invisible. They are simple to ask and frequently produce the highest-value moments in the entire conversation.

Health coverage
"How are you currently covering health insurance — employer plan, Marketplace, or no coverage? And do you know if you qualify for a subsidy?"
Health insurance is one of the largest cash flow line items for American households. A self-employed or independently insured client paying full Marketplace premiums without subsidy may be eligible for significant premium reduction. This question costs nothing to ask and frequently surfaces hundreds of dollars per month in unnecessary cost.
Disability
"Is anyone in your household living with a disability — yourself, a spouse, or a child?"
Surfaces potential SSDI eligibility, SSI eligibility, and ABLE account access. One of the most under-asked questions in a financial discovery conversation. Many disabled Americans have not applied or were denied without attempting an appeal.
Tax filing
"Do you file your taxes every year — even in years where you have little or no income?"
Filing is the trigger for the Earned Income Tax Credit and Child Tax Credit. A non-filing client with children or low-to-moderate income may be missing thousands of dollars per year in unclaimed refundable credits. The IRS does not contact eligible non-filers to notify them. This question is the highest-return question a professional can ask for clients in the applicable income range.
Employer benefits
"Are you using all of the benefits your employer offers — 401(k) match, FSA, HSA, dependent care FSA?"
Uncaptured employer 401(k) match is the most commonly missed cash flow lever in American households — it is a guaranteed 50–100% return on every dollar contributed, left on the table. HSA and FSA utilization surfaces tax-preferred dollars for healthcare spending that most clients are already incurring. This connects directly to Cash Flow, Growth, and Tax Efficiency simultaneously.
Student loan status
"Do you carry federal student loans — and do you know whether you are on an income-driven repayment plan or whether you might qualify for any forgiveness programs?"
Income-driven repayment (IDR) plans can dramatically reduce monthly federal student loan payments for clients whose loan balances are high relative to income. Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF) applies to many government and nonprofit employees who are not aware they qualify. This is one of the largest single restructuring opportunities available to American clients under 45.
